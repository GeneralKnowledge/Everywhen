# Everywhen: Causal timeline and ledger

**Document 2 of 3 in the story bible.** Companion documents: [01 Rules](01-rules.md) and [03 Character and revelation](03-character-and-revelation.md).

**Status:** Draft 1.1, after the continuity audit. This is the hidden truth in calendar order, with every significant object, message and phrase accounted for. If the novel contains a loop, the loop has to balance here.

## 1. Conventions

- **Y** is the year of the present action. Adam is 41 in Y. Years before it are written Y−4, Y−31 and so on.
- The present action runs through weeks **W1 to W14**, from autumn into winter of Y. W0 is his mother's funeral.
- Times use the 24-hour clock.
- **Fold times are counted from F+0**, the first words of the late shipping forecast (rules, R5a). At K2, K3 and T, F+0 is 00:48. At K1 it is **~00:33**, because before October 1995 the late forecast went out about 15 minutes earlier (rules, §8, research note 2). Barrow's watch therefore reads ~15 minutes behind Adam's clock throughout.
- **Midnight crossings are written in full.** Every fold happens after midnight, so each one has two dates:
  - the *night* it belongs to, which is how the characters talk about it;
  - the *calendar date* of the window, which is what timestamps show.

  For example, the break-up is "that Thursday" to Adam and Nell, but its fold window falls on **Friday**.
- The setting is provisionally Edinburgh. Adam's flat is in Marchmont, his mother's house is in Corstorphine, and Nell lives in Leith.

### How each fold meets its conditions

**F1 (shallow, so it needs (a) and (c) but no anchor; rules, R7):**

| Condition | Thursday end (finding it) | Friday end (losing it) |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Liminal state | Wrung out after a twelve-hour day and two months of grief, kneeling on the kitchen floor. | Acute dread, a few streets from Nell's door after four years. |

- **Thursday → Friday:** nothing he notices. When the pocket turns out to be empty, he simply isn't surprised.

- **Ordinary:** an unbroken everyday chain.
- **Fold:** the entry passes through a fold.
- **Loop, no origin:** a closed chain with no first instance (rules, R9).

### Why L4 and L18 are paired

The brief's provenance mechanic says the novel must make Adam tell *information with no apparent source* apart from *information with genuinely no source*. These two entries are that distinction made concrete.

- **L18** looks sourceless, because it appears in a 1993 letter before Nell ever says it. But it has an origin, which Adam can find: Nell's old email. It entered the Knot from outside.
- **L4** looks like it must have a source somewhere. It doesn't.

Adam traces both the same way, and gets two different answers.

## 7. Constraints for drafting

1. The K3 chapter must contain the §4 K3 column exactly and in order. Only three things may intrude: the blurred voice, the smell of collodion, and the sense of the room settling at F+24. Each must be something Adam can dismiss.
2. Barrow's notebook (W9) quotes the K1 column with her own timestamps. They are offset from Adam's clock, but the intervals match from F+0. The GP letter (W4) quotes only fragments.
3. Nell's account must match the K2 column, including the oddities:
   - the radio switched on;
   - the "evidence" set out on the table;
   - the phone held low;
   - being spoken of in the third person;
   - the earring line;
   - the text arriving while she packed.
4. The memo list on W6 Tue must show "Today 00:50" under the title.
5. The earring certainty (F1) must be pure conviction, with no image of the drain.
6. Anything a character says about "Thursday" must be clear about *which* Thursday it means, even when Adam isn't.
