# Everywhen: Causal timeline and ledger

**Document 2 of 3 in the story bible.** Companion documents: [01 Rules](01-rules.md) and [03 Character and revelation](03-character-and-revelation.md).

**Status:** Draft 1. This is the hidden truth, set out in calendar order, with every significant object, message and phrase accounted for. If the novel contains a loop, the loop has to balance here.

## 1. Conventions

- **Y** is the year of the present action. Adam is 41 in Y. Years before it are written Y−4, Y−31 and so on.
- The present action runs through weeks **W1 to W14**, from autumn into winter of Y. W0 is his mother's funeral.
- Times use the 24-hour clock. Every fold lines up on the late shipping forecast (R5).
- The setting is provisionally Edinburgh. Adam's flat is in Marchmont, his mother's house is in Corstorphine, and Nell lives in Leith.

## 2. The folds

The design uses only three folds, which is as little complexity as the story needs.

| Fold | Ends | Window | Depth | Lucid end | Gap ends |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **F1: The earring** | W1 Thu ~21:10 (Adam holding the earring) ↔ W1 Fri ~17:40 (his hand feeling for it in his pocket) | Seconds | Shallow | n/a | n/a. All that passes is certainty that he'll lose it. |
| **F2: The Knot** | **K1** Y−31, sleep-study room, Royal Hospital for Sick Children ↔ **K2** Y−4, a Thursday, the kitchen of Adam's flat, with Nell ↔ **K3** Y W6, Wednesday night into Thursday, the same kitchen, Adam alone | 00:48–01:12 | Deep | K3 | K1 and K2 |
| **F3: The memo** | **T** Y W6, Monday night into Tuesday, Adam in bed, phone in hand, radio on ↔ **K3** | 00:50–00:51 | Deep | K3 | T |

What makes each pair rhyme (R5):

- **F1:** his hand closed round the same small object, and dread.
- **F2:** the forecast, plus someone asking Adam where he is. In K1 Barrow asks *"Can you tell me where you are, Adam?"* In K2 Nell asks *"Where are you, Adam?"* In K3 he hears Barrow's question through the fold.
- **F3:** the forecast, insomnia, his phone in his hand, and thinking about Nell.

## 5. The causal ledger

Each entry records its type, its origin, its chain of custody (for matter) or its occurrences (for information), when Adam first encounters it, and its status. The status is one of:

- **Ordinary:** an unbroken everyday chain.
- **Fold:** the entry passes through a fold.
- **Loop, no origin:** a closed chain with no first instance (R9).

### Why L4 and L18 are paired

The brief's provenance mechanic says the novel must make Adam tell *information with no apparent source* apart from *information with genuinely no source*. These two entries are that distinction made concrete.

- **L18** looks sourceless, because it appears in a 1993 letter before Nell ever says it. But it has an origin, which Adam can find: Nell's old email. It entered the Knot from outside.
- **L4** looks like it must have a source somewhere. It doesn't.

Adam traces both the same way, and gets two different answers.

## 6. Balance check

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Does any object cross between moments? | No. Every physical artefact (L1, L2, L3, L12, L13, L16, L17) has an unbroken forward chain. |
| Does every content loop close? | L4, L5 and L9 close through the Knot. L18 enters from outside. |
| Is every K3 action motivated locally? | Yes. He records the memo and writes the note to "close the loops" (Theory B). He talks to himself because that is how he reasons. He texts Nell because he is sorry. He says "Everywhen" in answer to a voice, using Nell's word. |
| Does every gap end act out only what its circumstances allow? | Yes. K1 has no phone, so no memo or text, only gestures. K2 has no atlas, so the notepad stands in. |
| Do both accounts of the break-up survive? | Yes. Nell's account covers 00:48–01:12. Adam's covers the rest. Neither is false. |
| Does anything depend on the ending? | No. The causal puzzle closes at K3, which falls in Movement III, so the ending is free. See the variant in document 03, §7. |

## 7. Constraints for drafting

1. The chapter covering K3 (W6 Wed/Thu) must contain the §4 script exactly and in order. It should read as an ordinary night of obsessive reasoning. Barrow's voice and the collodion smell should be the only intrusions, and Adam should be able to dismiss them.
2. Barrow's 1993 transcript, when it finally appears in W9, must quote §4 column K1. The GP letter quotes only fragments of it.
3. Nell's account of the break-up must match §4 column K2, including the oddities: the dictating tone, being spoken of in the third person, the earring line, and the text arriving from the next room.
4. The memo list in W6 Tue must show "Today 00:50" under the title. That is the fair-play clue.
5. Under F1, the earring certainty must be pure conviction. It must not come with any image of the drain.
