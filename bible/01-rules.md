# Everywhen: Rules

**Document 1 of 3 in the story bible.** Companion documents: [02 Causal timeline and ledger](02-causal-timeline.md) and [03 Character and revelation](03-character-and-revelation.md).

**Status:** Draft 1.1, after the continuity audit. Covers development stages 1 to 3 (anomalies, competing explanations, causal structure). It's for the author only. Adam never puts this document together in the story, but a careful reader should be able to work out most of it.

**Markers used throughout the bible:**

- **[canon]** is decided for this draft. Changing it means updating the ledger.
- **[provisional]** is a working assumption the story is allowed to overturn.
- **[open]** is left undecided on purpose, usually to protect the ending.

All names and places are provisional working labels.

---

## 1. What is actually happening

Adam Rennick's life is a single, complete, self-consistent structure. Every moment of it is equally real, and each moment experiences itself as *now*.

Most of the time, moments that are neighbours in the structure are also neighbours on the calendar. That's why his life feels like a line. But a few moments that sit decades apart are neighbours in the structure too. When they are, they **share one stream of content**: whatever he says, does and perceives in one of them is said, done and perceived in all of them. Each body acts it out as far as its own circumstances allow.

Nothing travels and no message is sent. On a handful of nights, Adam has been in more than one moment at once, and the novel's anomalies are what those nights left behind.

He isn't learning to travel through time. He's discovering that he was never only in one place in it.

## 2. Author's vocabulary

These are working terms. None of them should appear in the prose until a character has earned the word.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Moment** | A stretch of Adam's life as it is lived from the inside: minutes, not days. |
| **Adjacency** | Two moments being neighbours in the structure, however far apart they are on the calendar. |
| **Fold** | An event in which adjacent moments share content. |
| **End** | Each moment taking part in a fold. |
| **Window** | How long a fold lasts. It is counted from **F+0**, the first words of the forecast (R5a). |
| **Lucid end / gap end** | The end that lives the fold knowingly and remembers it, and the end(s) that experience it as lost time. See R6. |
| **Deep fold** | A fold that shares words, actions and perceptions, and so produces gaps. |
| **Shallow fold** | A fold that shares only feeling or conviction. It produces no gap. |
| **Script** | The content of a deep fold: everything said and done during the window. |
| **The Knot** | The novel's one fold with three ends. It sits at the centre of the mystery. See the timeline, §4. |
| **Enactment** | How a particular body carries out the shared script with whatever is in front of it. |

## 3. The rules

**R1. There is one history. [canon]**
Nothing is ever changed. Every fold is already part of the history, and an "intervention" is the discovery of something that was always so.
*Consequence:* Adam can never prevent an anomaly. His attempts to prevent one become part of it (see Anomaly 1, the earring, and Anomaly 5, the memo).

**R2. Matter keeps calendar order. [canon]**
Objects only persist forwards through ordinary time. Every object in the ledger has an unbroken physical chain of custody.
*Consequence:* when an object seems to "come back", what you actually find is two objects: the original, made at the time, and a later copy. This is the backbone of the Movement IV contradiction.

**R3. Content is shared, but enactment is local. [canon]**
In a deep fold, every end shares the same words, intentions and perceptions. Each body carries them out with whatever is there.
*Consequences:*
- A ten-year-old's hand writes an adult's letterforms with a ten-year-old's motor control.
- A man with a phone records a voice memo. A boy with no phone holds his hand to his ear.
- Where an action can't be carried out at all, it doesn't happen at that end.

**R4. Adjacency is symmetric. [canon]**
No end is the sender. Content flows every way at once. The lucid end has local reasons for what it does, but those reasons were themselves supplied by the fold.
*Consequence:* the hunt for "who sent it" (Theory B) is the wrong question. It is locally useful, and globally mistaken.

**R5. Adjacency is rhyme. [provisional]**
Moments become adjacent when they rhyme closely enough on three things:
- **(a)** a liminal state: half-sleep, exhaustion, or acute emotion;
- **(b)** a shared sensory anchor, which for Adam is the late shipping forecast on Radio 4 and *Sailing By* before it;
- **(c)** a matching situation, such as a woman asking him where he is, or someone about to leave.

Calendar distance is irrelevant. All three conditions apply to **deep** folds. Shallow folds work differently (see R7).

**R5a. The broadcast opens a fold. It doesn't hold it open. [provisional]**
The anchor is a *recognition signal*: the moment every end hears the same thing at the same point in it. The fold opens on the first words of the forecast, and that moment is called **F+0**.

After that, the fold lasts as long as (a) and (c) hold at **every** end. It closes the moment any end breaks the rhyme. The broadcast itself can end while the fold carries on.

*Timing for drafting:* at K2, K3 and T, Radio 4's schedule is the same, so clock times line up (*Sailing By* at ~00:45, the forecast at 00:48 and running about 11 minutes to ~00:59, then the Radio 4 closedown and the switch to the World Service at 01:00). The fold opens at 00:48 (F+0) and runs 24 minutes. At K2 it closes at 01:12 (F+24), when Nell walks back into the kitchen carrying a bag. A woman *leaving* is a different situation from a woman *asking where he is*, so the rhyme breaks.

K1 lines up on F+0, **not on the clock**. If the 1993 late forecast went out at a different time, Barrow's timestamps are offset from Adam's by exactly that difference. That is consistent with the rules, and it's a gift to a careful reader. *[Research flag: confirm the late forecast's time in the early 1990s, and when Radio 4 began handing over to the World Service overnight.]*

**R6. Each fold has one lucid end; the others are gaps. [provisional]**
The lucid end is the moment whose own circumstances supply local reasons for the script. Adam lives that end fully and remembers it.

The gap ends experience nothing from the inside, just lost minutes. From the outside, he seems present, fluent and oddly certain, and he speaks "as if he were reading something out" or "dictating". A gap end may keep fragments that are encoded like a dream.

Being the lucid end doesn't make you the author (see R4).

**R7. Shallow folds pass conviction, not content. [provisional]**
A shallow fold involves no action and leaves no gap. It's felt as certainty, as déjà vu, or as dread with a date attached. Whether everyone has shallow folds is deliberately left **[open]** (see §8).

Shallow folds need **(a)** a liminal state and **(c)** a matching situation, but **not (b)**, the anchor. This is a consequence of the mechanism, not an exception made for the plot:

- The anchor exists to *synchronise* the ends. Shared words and actions need the ends lined up to the minute, or speech at one end would land mid-sentence at the other.
- A feeling has no duration that needs lining up. Without an anchor, a fold can carry only what needs no synchronising.

So a fold without an anchor is *always* shallow.

R8 still applies: Adam can no more summon a shallow fold than a deep one.

*For the reader:* none of this needs stating early. The earring is an anomaly, not a solution. It sets out no rule the climax relies on. Its explanation comes in Movement V, when Adam re-reads his life's déjà vu and presentiments as the faint end of the same phenomenon.

**R8. Perception is not control. [canon]**
Adam can't fold at will. He can arrange the conditions of R5, but he can't summon the fold. Understanding the structure gives him no power to change it (R1) and very little power to reach it.

**R9. Information can form a closed loop with no origin. [canon]**
A loop of content can have no first instance. The novel contains loops like this. They balance in the ledger, but they have no source. That absence is the floor of the mystery, not a hole in it.

**R10. Memory is physical and runs in calendar order. [canon]**
A brain remembers only what it lived, at the time it lived it. Adam can't remember the future. Any "knowledge from elsewhere" arrives either as conviction (R7) or as things he did in a gap and doesn't remember (R6).
*Consequence:* this is why the phenomenon has stayed hidden inside his life for thirty-one years. It explains why Theory A is so persuasive, and why it is partly true.

**R11. Folds can share an end without merging. [provisional]**
Content passes only between the ends of the same fold. F3 (the memo fold) shares one end, K3, with the Knot. Tuesday's Adam therefore acts out only F3's single minute, the memo, and receives nothing from K1 or K2. *[Alternative to weigh: make T a fourth end of the Knot for one minute. It's tidier, but it would mean Tuesday's body also received the Knot's perceptions.]*

## 3a. What is shared and what is local

This is the core of R3, made precise. **It applies only to Adam.** Other people are never in a fold: Nell, Barrow and Jo perceive and remember normally, and they interpret what they see for themselves.

| | Shared across every end of a deep fold | Local to each end |
|---|---|---|
| **Speech** | Adam's words, exactly | The tone they land with, the person who hears them and what that person takes them to mean |
| **Action** | The *intention* (record, write, fold it, put it away, send) | The objects to hand, so the action is carried out with what is there, or not at all |
| **Perception** | Fragments only (a voice, a smell, an image), at low fidelity | Everything Adam is actually looking at. At the lucid end, fragments reaching him from other ends feel like tiredness or imagination. At the gap ends, they may survive as dream images. |
| **Memory** | Nothing. Memory is physical (R10). | The lucid end remembers its own experience. The gap ends remember nothing, or keep dream fragments. |
| **Knowledge** | Only what is *in* the words, actions and perceptions, as carried out at that end | Everything else Adam knows at each end |

**A worked example: the question "Where are you, Adam?"** At F+10 two women ask him where he is: Nell at K2 and Barrow at K1. At K3, the lucid end, Adam hears both voices blur into one: *"Can you tell me where you are, Adam?"* For a moment it sounds like Nell, and then he's not sure. He answers aloud, with Nell's word: *"Everywhen."* The single answer is spoken at all three ends, so each woman hears a reply to her own question.

## 4. What the rules forbid (drafting checklist)

- [ ] No object moves between moments. Every artefact has a physical chain in the ledger.
- [ ] No established fact changes. Revelations reinterpret facts. They never revise them.
- [ ] Every fold states which R5 conditions it meets, at every end. Deep folds need (a), (b) and (c). Shallow folds need (a) and (c) (see R7).
- [ ] No fold happens at will, and no fold arrives to rescue the plot. A fold at the climax needs all three R5 conditions set up beforehand.
- [ ] No end of a fold contains content that the lucid end wouldn't plausibly say or do for its own local reasons.
- [ ] No new rule is introduced after Movement IV. Everything the climax uses must be visible on the page by then.
- [ ] No anomaly lacks a ledger entry.

## 5. The three theories, and where each is locally right

The brief's rule is that an early explanation should be locally correct but globally incomplete. This design makes all three theories true at some level.

| Theory | What it gets right | Where it breaks | What it is actually seeing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A: it's psychological** | Adam really did produce every artefact himself. His memory really has gaps, which a doctor once investigated as absence seizures. Priming really does shape the Week 6 argument, because he steers it. | Documents written in 1993 contain words he hasn't said yet (Anomaly 4). His EEG was normal *during* a gap (Anomaly 7). | The gaps (R6, R10). |
| **B: it's a causal loop** | Closed loops of content are real and they balance: the sentence, the word, the memo's title. Adam really does make the memo and the note. | The memo's background radio dates the original to Tuesday (Anomaly 5). The note was written by a child's hand (Anomaly 2). Nothing ever travelled. | The script, and R9. |
| **C: his model of existence is wrong** | Moments aren't self-contained, and some nights are the same night. | Nothing breaks it, but the novel never has to prove it as metaphysics. It can remain an interpretation (ending requirement 3). | The structure itself. |

## 6. How this draft uses the brief's five mechanics

| Mechanic (from the brief) | Where it lives in this design |
|---|---|
| 1. Temporal adjacency | R5. The Knot joins a night in 1993, a night four years ago and a night in Week 6. The shipping forecast is the recurring "peculiar detail". |
| 2. Causality as a constraint, not a direction | R1, R4 and R9. The Knot script has no first line. |
| 3. Information has a provenance problem | R9 and the ledger. Two phrases are deliberately paired. *"You're hiding in it"* has a findable origin (Nell's own phrase, attested in an old email). *"I already know how this ends"* has none. Adam has to learn to tell them apart. |
| 4. Perception is not control | R6 and R8. Adam's attempt to fold deliberately in Movement V fails. |
| 5. The self as a continuity problem | R3 and R6, made concrete. Who said *"I already know how this ends"* to Nell four years ago: the man in the kitchen that night, or the man in the same kitchen in Week 6? The answer decides who ended the relationship. |

## 7. The brief's design principles, and how this draft enforces them

| Principle | Enforcement |
|---|---|
| Every anomaly has a traceable history | Every anomaly maps to ledger entries in document 02. |
| Every intervention has consequences | Preventing the earring's loss causes it, and the loss reconnects him with Nell. Obeying the memo means he never asks about Thursday, and Nell takes the Lisbon job. |
| Information and matter are different | R2 and R3. The two Movement IV/V proofs (the forecast and the handwriting) are both cases of matter staying put while content moves. |
| Memory is evidence, not proof | R10. Both Adam's and Nell's accounts of the break-up are accurate memories of incomplete experiences. |
| A paradox requires accounting | R9, plus the ledger's list of loops with no origin. |
| The rules come before the solution | The forbids checklist in §4. Every rule the climax uses is visible by the end of Movement IV. |
| Adam can't simply call something impossible | At every anomaly, document 03 records what he concludes: that the evidence is false, the model is wrong, or there's a mechanism he has missed. |

## 8. Open questions

- **Why Adam? [open]** There are three live options, and the novel shouldn't close any of them before stage 4:
  1. He is unique.
  2. Everyone has shallow folds (R7, déjà vu), and a few people have deep ones.
  3. Nell has them too, and her own lost minutes are part of the evidence.
- **Did his mother know? [open]** She kept the GP letter and shelved the atlas. Did she ever read the note? Did she recognise her grown son's handwriting in it? There's a strong emotional opportunity here for stage 4.
- **Is the Knot the only deep fold? [provisional: yes]** Adding more would be complexity the story doesn't need.
- **What is the structure? [open by design]** The causal facts resolve. Their metaphysical meaning doesn't have to.
- **The missing notepad page. [open]** At the Y−4 end of the Knot, Adam wrote the sentence on the kitchen notepad. Nobody has found that page. It is held in reserve as a possible late discovery.

### Research flags

- The late-night Radio 4 shipping forecast: its time in the early 1990s and today, the role of *Sailing By*, and whether past forecast texts can be looked up in a public archive.
- What an EEG shows during absence seizures (classically 3 Hz spike-and-wave), so that a *normal* EEG during a gap reads as meaningful.
- Forensic handwriting examination: whether a child writing adult letterforms (line quality, pressure, size, tremor) is distinguishable, and how an examiner would phrase that conclusion.
- Voice-memo apps: how a typed title and the system date appear in the recordings list.
- W. E. H. Stanner's use of "everywhen" (in the 1953 essay "The Dreaming"). This is the real-world origin of the word, and it's the ordinary explanation available to Theory A. See the cultural note below.

### Cultural note: the word *everywhen*

Stanner coined *everywhen* in an attempt to convey, in English, something of how Aboriginal Australian people understand the Dreaming. The word has a real intellectual and cultural history, and the novel needs to respect it:

- **The fold mechanics are this novel's invention.** They must never be presented as an Indigenous Australian worldview, nor as a "discovery" of one. Nobody in the book, including the narrator, should claim the Dreaming "explains" Adam.
- **The word can keep its mystery without pretending it has no history.** If Stanner's book on his father's shelf is how Adam's family came by the word, Adam can find that out. When he does, he should come to understand it as a word someone made to describe *other people's* understanding of time, not as a key to his own condition. That distance is honest, and it fits the book's theme of the limits of explanation.
- **The research pass should read Stanner properly**, along with Aboriginal writing on the Dreaming and later criticism of his framing, before any scene leans on the word's origin. Consider a sensitivity read if that scene stays.
