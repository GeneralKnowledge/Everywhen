## 1. Cast (provisional)

| Name | Role | What they believe at the start |
|---|---|---|
| **Adam Rennick**, 41 | A loss adjuster. His job is to reconstruct what happened from damage, receipts and contradictory statements: methodical, observant and quietly proud of it. He sleeps badly, with Radio 4 on. He has had "lapses" since childhood and hides them. | That the past is settled, and that careful investigation can settle any dispute about it. That Nell left him. |
| **Nell Imrie**, 39 | His partner from Y−6 to Y−4. A garden designer with no garden of her own. Warm, direct, quick to name what she sees ("you're hiding in it"). Now lives in Leith. | That Adam ended it, coldly, with a sentence she has never forgotten. |

| **Helen Barrow**, 70s | A retired EEG technician who ran Adam's sleep study in Y−31. Precise, kind, and has wondered about the boy for thirty years. | That something happened in that room which her notes describe and her training can't explain. |

| **A colleague** *(unnamed)* | The voice of Theory A: a fraud investigator who has seen every way people fool themselves. Could also be the document examiner in W8. | That there's always an ordinary explanation. |

> **The governing rule for §§1.1–1.6:** the fold explains why their memories conflict. It does **not** explain away their problems. Without the Knot they would still have broken up, within months, for the reasons below. What the Knot decided was *how* it ended and *who each of them believes ended it*, not *whether* it would end.

### 1.1 Nell, apart from Adam

**What she wants is a place she has chosen, and the time to watch it grow.**

- Nell grew up in rented flats in Dundee and moved seven times before she was fifteen. Her father was in and out of work, and her mother restless.
- She designs gardens for a living and almost never sees one mature. Clients sell up, budgets end, and she moves on to the next job.
- To her, a garden is a promise that someone will still be there in ten years. She has never made that promise on her own behalf.

**Her flaw is that she forces the conversation before it's ready.** She names things early and sets deadlines on feelings, because to her, waiting feels like being left. She would rather know the worst on Thursday than live with a maybe until spring. This makes her brave, and sometimes unfair: she asks for an answer to a question the other person hasn't finished thinking about.

**After Adam (Y−4 to Y):** she spent two years with Callum, a kind man with a house in Fife and a large garden. He asked her to move in. She said no, and she couldn't explain why to him, though she could to herself: it would have been *his* garden. That detail matters. Her want is not "a garden" or "a man". It's something that is hers by choice.

**Why Lisbon matters:** the offer is to lead the planting for a ten-year restoration of a terraced public garden *(fictional; to be specified)*, with a small flat with a walled yard thrown in.
- It would be the first garden she sees grow up.
- It's the first thing she has wanted that doesn't depend on anyone choosing her.
- That's why it frightens her, and why she wanted to tell Adam in person.

**What she wanted from Thursday (W6):** *not* to ask. Four years ago she asked whether they had a future, and the answer she heard was *"I already know how this ends."* She won't be the one asking again. She wanted to tell him about Lisbon and see whether **he** would ask her to stay. The memo tells him not to ask. The relationship's oldest pattern (she asks, he holds back) has been turned into a piece of evidence that he obeys.

### 1.2 Adam, apart from Nell

**What he wants is to be the one who stays.**

- His father left when Adam was eight, without an explanation that anyone ever gave him.
- Adam's whole adult life is an answer to that: a job in explaining, a flat he has owned for eight years, a diary of facts kept every night.
- His private account of himself is that he is not his father. He has believed that Nell's leaving proves it: *she* went, and *he* stayed.

**The flaw (from the brief):** he assumes that if he can understand something completely, he'll know how to feel about it. In practice, he treats feelings as claims to be assessed. Bring him a complaint and he'll investigate it and come back with findings. He hardly ever says what he wants, because a want can't be verified.

**What the mystery costs him in particular:** Movement V doesn't only tell him that time is strange. It tells him that **he ended it**: that, by his own account of himself, he is the one who left. The Knot attacks the identity he has built in reply to his father. That is the personal stake under the puzzle.

### 1.3 Why it was failing before the Knot

These problems are all real, and none of them is caused by a fold.

| Problem | His side | Her side |
|---|---|---|
| **Who asks** | He doesn't ask for anything, so she can't give him anything. | She asks for everything, and then hears his silence as an answer. |
| **The future** | For two years he put off every decision ("after Mum's hip", "when the market settles"), gathering more information. | She heard each delay as a quiet no. |
| **His mother** | Margaret's illness began in Y−5. He used looking after her as a reason, and sometimes as a shelter. | She liked Margaret, and resented being the person his duties were always set against. |
| **The pregnancy scare (Y−5)** *[provisional]* | Relief, when the test came back negative. He never mentioned it. | She saw the relief, and never forgot it. |
| **His absences** | He has hidden his lapses from her and calls them nothing. | She took them for emotional withdrawal. Sometimes she was right, and sometimes it was something else. Her *"Where are you, Adam?"* at K2 is a question she had asked many times before. |
| **The kitchen** | He loves the swallow tiles, which were the first thing he saw when he viewed the flat. | She wanted to retile. *"Your swallows"* became a joke, and then not one. |
| **The radio at night** | It's how he sleeps. | It shut her out. That's why switching it on mid-argument at K2 felt deliberate to her. |

**What the Knot changed:** the Thursday argument at Y−4 was going to end the relationship anyway. Nell had come to put the question one last time, with new earrings to make it feel like an occasion and not an ambush. Without the fold, Adam would most likely have hedged, and she would have gone within weeks. The fold made it quick, put the words in his mouth and left each of them sure that the other was the one who chose.

### 1.4 What Adam loved about her, and what was hard about him

**What he loved about her:**
- She says the thing.
- She notices the physical world: the Latin names of plants, which way the weather is turning, the state of a hinge.
- She got him out of the house.
- She laughed at his lists, and then used them.
- She was the only person who ever asked him where he was, and meant it as an invitation rather than an accusation.

**What made living with him hard:**
- Being investigated instead of answered.
- Never knowing what he wanted.
- Losing him to the radio, to his mother and to the lapses he wouldn't name.
- Feeling she had to argue her own feelings like a claim, with evidence.

### 1.5 What each would count as reconciliation, and why it might not be possible

| | What would count | Why it might not be possible |
|---|---|---|
| **Nell** | To be asked. To hear Adam say what he wants, without evidence or an explanation attached. | The truth about the fold *is* an explanation. If he tells her, he risks being exactly what she accused him of: hiding in it. |
| **Adam** | To be understood: to have her accept that he didn't mean it, and that the words were tender. Reconciliation as acquittal. | It's the wrong want. And it isn't even true. In Week 6 he *was* letting her go: *"You should go somewhere with a garden"* was an apology and a release at once. The words were his, at both ends. |
| **Both** | To put down what each has been holding against the other. | The problems in §1.3 are real and still there. Lisbon is real and good for her, and asking her to stay could be the same taking in a new form. Even if she believes the fold, believing it doesn't mend anything. |

These are conditions, not endings. Each of the three ending directions in §7 is a different answer to the same question: can Adam ask Nell for something without first proving he's entitled to it?

### 1.6 Kept open on purpose

- **The mother thread. [open, with a direction]** All his life, Adam has taken the kitchen dream (Anomaly 6) to be his mother packing his father's bag, throwing him out. He has held his father's leaving against her ever since. In her papers, his father's letters show the father left by choice. And the woman in the dream was Nell, packing her own bag, in a kitchen where the man she was leaving was Adam. *He has blamed his mother for something he did himself.* She is dead, so the apology he owes her can't be given. **Still open:** whether Margaret read the atlas note, and whether she recognised her grown son's handwriting in it.
- **Does Nell have gaps? [open]** There are three options:
  - **(a)** None. She is the clean outside witness. This is the simplest option.
  - **(b)** Shallow folds only. For example, at W6 Thu 01:10 she "already knew" what the text would say before she opened it.
  - **(c)** Deep folds. *Not recommended:* it expands the cosmology, which this pass has agreed not to do.

  My recommendation is (a) or (b). Leave the choice until the opening is drafted.
- **Helen Barrow's stake.** In Y−31 she reported the episode honestly. The consultant softened it to "confabulation", and she let that stand. For thirty years she has wondered whether staying quiet was cowardice or kindness. Meeting Adam is how she settles it. *[A minor thread. Keep it small.]*

## 2. The break-up night: three accounts

The relationship at the centre of the book is built on this night, and it is the brief's emotional question in its sharpest form: *"If the past I remember is not the past we shared, what exactly have I been holding against you?"*

| | Adam's account | Nell's account | What happened (see document 02, §4) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Who ended it** | Nell did. She stood there with a bag and said *"Then I'll go."* | Adam did. He said *"I already know how this ends"*, told her to go somewhere with a garden, and then *texted* it to her from the next room. | Both. From 00:48 to 01:12, in the early hours of Friday, Adam was in a gap, and his mouth spoke the Week 6 script. Nell heard it as a verdict and went to pack. Adam came back to find her leaving. |
| **His manner** | Exhausted, trying to listen. He put the radio on to calm things down. | Absent. *"You turned the radio ON."* He spoke "as if he were reading something out", and about her in the third person. | Both accurate. The second describes the gap. |
| **Her evidence** | — | The 01:10 text, which she kept. The earring line: *"you knew I'd lost it."* The word *everywhen*. | Accurate memory of K2. |
| **His evidence** | His 01:30 diary entry. Jo's account of Nell's 01:20 call (*"I've told him I'm going"*). | — | Accurate memory of everything outside the gap. |
| **What each holds against the other** | That she walked out on something he was trying to save. | That he dismissed her, and was cruel about the earring she had bought for that night. | That the words came from a man four years in the future, alone in an empty kitchen and rehearsing an apology, which is tender and also a release. They were his at both ends. The argument that came before them was real, and so are the problems behind it (§1.3). |

**The design rule here:** Nell isn't secretly responsible for anything, and she doesn't hold the whole explanation. Her conflicting experience is real evidence of the phenomenon, and the novel has to treat it that way.

## 3. The anomalies

There are seven anomalies, in the order the reader meets them. Each one follows the brief's template:

- what Adam initially concludes;
- the best ordinary alternative;
- the evidence that tells them apart;
- what remains unexplained.

**Clue type** follows the brief: I is an observable anomaly, II is a causal link and III is a category error.

---

### Anomaly 1: The green earring

**Introduced:** W1 (Movement I). **Reclassified:** W9. **Ledger:** L1, L1c, Fold F1.

**What happens.** The fridge breaks. Moving it, Adam finds a green glass earring he has never seen before. He knows, with a certainty he can't account for, that he will lose it tomorrow and won't feel it go. To prevent that, he decides to return it to its obvious owner, Nell, in person. This is the first time he has contacted her in four years. On the way, it slips through a hole in his coat lining and out through a grating on Leith Walk. He arrives with nothing to give her, which looks like a pretext.

**Clue type:** I (the object), then II (preventing the loss caused it).

| | |
|---|---|
| **Adam's initial interpretation** | Grief and anxiety. His mother has just died, and he has been primed to expect loss. The certainty is a feeling, and the loss is coincidence, or carelessness brought on by anxiety. |
| **Best alternative** | The same, plus self-fulfilment: if you're sure you'll lose something, you check your pocket constantly and that's how you lose it. |
| **Distinguishing evidence** | Little at this stage, which is right for Movement I. The only odd point is how specific it was: *"tomorrow, and I won't feel it go."* |
| **Unexplained at first** | Why he had never seen it, if it was Nell's. Answered in W3: she bought it that afternoon, for that conversation. |
| **Relationship consequence** | It brings Nell back into his life, and opens with an awkward lie he didn't tell. |
| **Truth** | The earring's history is entirely ordinary. The *certainty* came from a shallow fold (R7). The prevention is the cause (R1). This also introduces the book's quiet thesis: the first impossible thing is a feeling, and feelings are the one kind of evidence that he never logs. |
| **Fair-play clue** | In W3, Nell asks: *"How did you know it was under the fridge? You said that, you know. That night."* |

### Anomaly 2: The note in the atlas

**Introduced:** W2 (Movement I). **Reclassified:** W8 (the handwriting) and W9 (Barrow's notebook). **Ledger:** L2, L3, L4.

**What happens.** Clearing his childhood bedroom, Adam finds his old school atlas. Folded inside is a sheet of 1990s exercise paper. Written on it in pencil, in **his own adult handwriting**, is the sentence *"I already know how this ends."* He has never written it.

**Clue type:** I, then III. The note was never a message.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Adam's initial interpretation** | A forgery, or a joke. Or he wrote it as a teenager and his hand hasn't changed as much as he thinks. |
| **Best alternative** | He wrote it himself, years later, during one of his lapses. His mother kept the atlas, but the room wasn't sealed. |
| **Distinguishing evidence** | The paper matches his primary-school homework book, and that brand was discontinued in Y−28. In W3 the sentence turns up in Nell's account. In W4 the GP letter says that during the sleep study the boy "wrote a short note which he would not show me." He doesn't connect this to the note (fair-play clue). |
| **Unexplained after Movement III** | Theory B says he must write it in the future and somehow send it back. So, in W6, he writes it himself and puts it in the atlas, which leaves two notes in the same book. |
| **The break (W8)** | A document examiner compares the two. The letterforms are the same, but the line quality, pressure, size and tremor in the original are a child's. *"Your handwriting, held by a ten-year-old's hand."* Nothing was sent. The boy wrote it (R3). |
| **Relationship consequence** | The sentence Nell has carried for four years existed thirty-one years before she heard it. |
| **Truth** | It was written at K1, from the script. It is the clearest single image of R2 and R3: matter stays where it is, and content is shared. |

### Anomaly 3: The conversation Nell remembers

**Introduced:** first spark W1 Fri, on Nell's landing ("The night you ended it" / "The night you left"). Full collision W3 (Movement I into II). **Evidence for her account:** W4. **Evidence for his:** W5. **Both accounts reconciled:** W9 to W10. **Ledger:** L4, L5, L10, L11, L17.

**What happens.** At coffee, Nell says in passing that he ended it. Adam is certain she did. Her version includes things he would never say: *"I already know how this ends"* (the sentence from the atlas), a remark about a garden, the word *everywhen*, and the fact that he knew her earring was under the fridge.

**Clue type:** I (two witnesses disagree), then II (her account contains the note's sentence), then III (both accounts are true).

| | |
|---|---|
| **Adam's initial interpretation** | She has rewritten the night to make herself the one who was left. She's mixing it up with another argument. |
| **Best alternative (hers)** | He was drinking, he's rewritten it, and that's what he does. |
| **Evidence for her (W4)** | His old phone has a text sent at **Fri** 01:10, Y−4, in the early hours after "that Thursday": *"I already know how this ends. You should go somewhere with a garden."* He has no memory of sending it. Theory A: he'd been drinking, or dissociating. |
| **Evidence for him (W5)** | His diary entry, written at 01:30 that night in fountain pen, says *"N. said she's going. Didn't argue. Don't understand."* Jo confirms that Nell's 01:20 call said *"I've told him I'm going."* |
| **The escalation (W6 into W7)** | At 01:10 on Thursday of W6, Nell receives a text identical, word for word, to the one from four years ago. It's in the same thread, from the same number, at the same minute past midnight. She takes it as cruelty. Then, in W7, she sends him the screenshot: *"Is this a joke?"* That makes her a witness to the anomaly, not just a source for it. |
| **Unexplained until Movement V** | How a man can end a relationship without experiencing doing so. |
| **Relationship consequence** | This is the main thread. Adam investigates Nell behind her back in W5 to test her account, which is the flaw in action. She finds out, and it's part of why she says he is *"hiding in it."* |
| **Truth** | Nell's account covers the gap, from 00:48 to 01:12 on Friday. Adam's covers everything else. The words she has held against him were spoken by him, from Week 6. |

### Anomaly 4: The stranger who knows

**Introduced:** W4 (Movement II). **Fully revealed:** W9. **Ledger:** L12, L13, L14, L18.

**What happens.** A woman Adam doesn't remember ever meeting, Helen Barrow, sends a condolence card. When they meet she asks, *"Did you ever find the earring?"* She knows the word *everywhen*. In his mother's papers, the consultant's 1993 letter to the GP records the ten-year-old Adam speaking fluently about "a green earring… under the fridge", saying *"she says I'm hiding in it"*, and writing a note. The letter is dated thirty-one years before any of these things happened.

**Clue type:** I (a contemporaneous document), then II (the letter predicts Nell's words), then III (the transcript *is* his Week 6 monologue).

| | |
|---|---|
| **Adam's initial interpretation** | Barrow knew his mother, and his mother talked. Or Barrow is a fantasist reading the death notices. |
| **Best alternative** | Vague details matched after the fact. A green earring is common, and a fridge is where small things end up. The phrases are generic. |
| **Distinguishing evidence** | The carbon copy comes from his mother's papers, so it is independent of Barrow and dated Y−31. His mother died before he found the earring, so she couldn't have told anyone. In W6, Nell says *"you're hiding in it"*, and the letter turns out to have predicted it (Movement III). |
| **The trap (Theory A's best move)** | Adam read *"she says I'm hiding in it"* in W4, and then steered the W6 argument towards it. Did he cause it? Then he finds the Y−5 email, which shows it was already Nell's phrase (L18). That has an origin. The sentence doesn't (L4). |
| **The revelation (W9)** | Barrow finds her original notebook, a verbatim transcript of the boy's twenty-four minutes. Reading it, Adam recognises **his own monologue from Wednesday night in Week 6**, line for line, including the answer *"Everywhen"* to a question he heard and dismissed as tiredness. |
| **Fair-play clue** | The K3 chapter contains Barrow's voice asking *"Can you tell me where you are, Adam?"* and the smell of collodion. A reader who remembers the GP letter can get there before he does. |
| **Truth** | K1 is a gap end of the Knot. Barrow transcribed the script. Her timestamps are offset from Adam's clock by the difference between the 1993 and present-day broadcast times, but the intervals match his Wednesday night minute for minute from the start of the forecast. A careful reader with a stopwatch can confirm it. |

### Anomaly 5: The voice memo, "Thursday"

**Introduced:** W6 Tue (Movement II into III). **The contradiction:** W7 (Movement IV). **Ledger:** L6, L7, L8, L9. This is the brief's own miniature example, built out in full.

**What happens.** On the Tuesday morning there is a voice memo on his phone, in his own voice: *"Don't ask her about Thursday."* In the list of recordings, the title reads **"Thursday"**, and Adam takes it as a date. Nell has asked to meet on Thursday because she has something to tell him. He obeys the memo: he doesn't ask what it's about, and he puts her off.

**Clue type:** I, then II (it becomes the test of the loop theory), then the **Movement IV contradiction**.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Adam's initial interpretation** | A glitch, or a hoax. Then he checks: it was recorded on his own phone. That's when he forms the loop hypothesis. He will record it himself, on Thursday, and it will somehow come back. |
| **Best alternative** | He recorded it himself, half asleep. He'd been brooding about Nell. The title is just a word he typed. |
| **Movement III (the test)** | The loop model predicts what comes next: the GP letter's line, *"she says I'm hiding in it"*, will be said. On Wednesday, Nell says it. Satisfied that the model holds, Adam spends Wednesday night "closing the loops". At 00:50 he records the memo deliberately and titles it "Thursday" to match. |
| **The contradiction (W7)** | Both recordings have Radio 4 in the background. Adam checks the forecast text against the public archive. **The original's forecast is Tuesday's, not Thursday's.** It was recorded on Tuesday at 00:50, in his flat, by him. Nothing came back from Thursday. The loop model needs a sender, and there wasn't one. |
| **What it exposes** | An assumption he has never examined: that he was only ever in one moment at a time. |
| **What it revives** | Theory A (sleep-recording), which leads to a worse question: then how did Tuesday's Adam know the words? |
| **Fair-play clue** | Under the title, in small grey type, the list says **"Today 00:50"**. A careful reader can see on Tuesday that "Thursday" is a typed title, not a date. The shipping forecast has been in the book since its first page. |
| **The other meaning** | When Adam records the memo deliberately on Wednesday night, after Nell has accused him of hiding in his investigation of the break-up, "Thursday" could just as well mean *that* Thursday, four years ago. Was the memo protecting him, steering him, or simply keeping a self-consistent history intact? **[open]**. The novel should never settle this. |
| **Relationship consequence** | Because he obeys it, he never asks, and Nell takes the Lisbon job. The memo is evidence, an intervention and an expression of his character all at once. |
| **Optional plant** | The old phone (W4) holds a Y−4 memo titled "Thursday". It's too muffled to make out at first. Re-heard in W9, it's the same sentence. |

### Anomaly 6: The kitchen in his childhood dream

**Introduced:** first mentioned in passing in W2. **Verified:** W9 (Movement V). **Ledger:** L15, L16.

**What happens.** Since childhood, Adam has carried a single vivid image: a kitchen in yellow lamplight, white tiles with a small blue swallow on every fifth one, and a woman with a bag, wearing one green earring. He has always taken it to be the night his father left. At some level he has held it against his mother.

**Clue type:** III. A childhood memory contains a detail he only came across as an adult.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Adam's initial interpretation** | It's an early memory, and the details have blurred with time. |
| **Best alternative** | Memory reconsolidation. This is a real and well-documented effect: every time he recalls the image, he rebuilds it from what's around him now, so the tiles came from his own kitchen. |
| **Distinguishing evidence** | Two things together. "My Worst Dream", a school essay from Y−30 found in his mother's house, describes the swallow tiles in a child's hand. And a trade catalogue shows the pattern wasn't manufactured until Y−12. Together they mean the description predates the tiles, which reconsolidation can't explain. |
| **Unexplained** | Nothing, once the Knot is understood: the boy saw the K2 kitchen through the fold. |
| **Relationship consequence** | There are two. The woman in the dream was never his mother, which reopens what he has held against her. And it was Nell, on the night he believes she left him. |
| **Fair-play clue** | The swallow tiles are described in the first kitchen scene of W1, when he moves the fridge. |

### Anomaly 7: The gaps

**Lifelong. Mentioned:** W1 onwards, as "lapses". **Reclassified:** W9 (Movement V). **Ledger:** cross-cutting, R6.

**What happens.** Since he was nine, Adam has had brief stretches of lost time. His mother called it *"going away"*. Doctors suspected absence seizures, and he was told he would grow out of them. He mostly did. He hides the ones that are left, and has built a careful life around that.

**Clue type:** III, the defining category error of the book. The evidence was never pointing at something wrong with his brain. **Adam himself is the evidence.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Adam's initial interpretation** | A neurological quirk. Embarrassing, and nothing to do with the anomalies. |
| **Best alternative** | Absence epilepsy or a dissociative condition, which would explain the memo, the text and the note without anything strange happening: he does things and doesn't remember them. *This is Theory A at its strongest.* |
| **Distinguishing evidence** | Barrow's notebook records that **the EEG stayed normal during the episode**: no spike-and-wave activity, fully fluent speech. An absence seizure doesn't look like that. And what he did in the gaps contains information he couldn't yet have had. |
| **Unexplained, and left that way** | Why him (document 01, §8). |
| **Relationship consequence** | He has to tell Nell. Telling her means admitting that he has hidden something from her all along, and that the evidence for his "crazy theory" is something he concealed. |
| **Truth** | Every lapse he has ever had was a near-fold: a fold that began to open and failed, with nothing shared (rules, R12). K1 and K2 are the two that opened all the way. He has been, in Nell's word, *everywhen* all his life. Mostly it has been an almost. |

## 4. Revelation order by movement

| Movement | Weeks | Question | Anomalies in play | Adam's working model at the end of the movement | Emotional turn |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I. The discrepancy** | W1–W3 | What actually happened? | 1, 2 and 3 introduced; 6 and 7 mentioned in passing | **A.** Grief, coincidence, a misremembering ex. He starts keeping records. | Nell is back. Old resentment surfaces on both sides. |
| **II. The pattern** | W4–W6 Tue | What connects them? | 3 (the text, the diary), 4 (Barrow and the letter), 5 (the memo) | Barrow's letter links the earring, the note and Nell's account. **B begins.** | He investigates Nell behind her back, and she finds out. |
| **III. The mechanism** | W6 | What kind of process could produce this? | 5, 4 (the prediction comes true) | **B confirmed.** The loop predicts "hiding in it", and it happens. Wednesday night (K3): he "closes the loops". | He obeys the memo, doesn't ask, and loses Thursday. |
| **IV. The contradiction** | W7–W8 | Why does the correct explanation fail? | 5 (the forecast), 2 (the handwriting), 3 (the identical text) | **B breaks.** Nothing was sent. | **V. The reclassification** | W9–W11 | What if he misunderstood the thing being investigated? | 4 (the notebook), 6 (the tiles), 7 (the gaps); everything reread | **C.** Certain nights are the same night, and he has always been in more than one. The words Nell heard four years ago were his, from Week 6. He tries to fold deliberately and fails. | *What exactly have I been holding against you?* Directed at Nell, and at his mother. |

## 7. The ending: requirements met, direction left open

The brief's three requirements are met by design. None of them depends on choosing an ending.

1. **The principal causal mystery resolves.** It closes at K3, in Movement III, and is fully reconstructable by Movement V. Movement VI needs no new causal fact.
2. **Adam's understanding of himself changes.** He ended the relationship he blamed Nell for ending, from a moment four years after it. His "lapses" were never a defect.
3. **The nature of reality can stay open.** The novel never needs to confirm Theory C as metaphysics. Stanner's book leaves an ordinary path for the word *everywhen*, and the question of why it happens to him stays open.

The situation in Movement VI is that Nell leaves for Lisbon in W14, and Adam knows everything above. None of the three directions in the brief has been chosen. Here is what each would need from this design:

| Direction | How it would play in this design | What it needs |
|---|---|---|
| **The closed circle** | **A variant:** move K3 from W6 to the climax, so that the night in the kitchen with Nell present *is* the K3 end, and the words Nell heard four years ago are what he says to her now. The loop would close at the very end. | The script in document 02, §4 moves to the climax, and Movement III loses its lucid-end chapter. That's a significant structural change, so it should be decided before any drafting. |
| **The altered understanding** | No temporal event at the climax. Adam has to decide whether to tell Nell that the man who ended it four years ago was him, and *who he was then*. Then, whether to ask her to stay, knowing he can't prove any of it. | Nothing new. The design already supports it. |
| **The quiet revelation** | An ordinary moment: the forecast comes on in a room with Nell. One of them recognises the rhythm of the night, or Nell recognises something, and the reader rereads the whole book. | A decision about whether Nell can fold (document 01, §8). The missing notepad page (document 02, §4) is held in reserve for this. |

**My recommendation for now:** keep K3 in Week 6 as drafted. It means the causal puzzle is finished before the personal question begins, which matches the brief's point that understanding the rules should *force* a choice rather than *be* the climax. It also leaves all three endings available, apart from the closed-circle variant, which would need K3 moved to the climax.
